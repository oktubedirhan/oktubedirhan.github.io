    The Planning Fallacy

    1Ask a group of students how long their next assignment will take, and most will give an answer that later proves wildly inaccurate. Psychologists call this tendency the planning fallacy: the habit of underestimating the time a task requires, even when we know that similar tasks have overrun in the past. First described in 1979 by Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky, it has since been documented in settings ranging from kitchen renovations to billion-dollar construction projects.

    2Why do we keep falling prey to it? (I) One explanation is that people focus on the task in front of them and imagine it going smoothly. (II) In a well-known Canadian study, psychology students predicted that they would finish their theses in about 34 days; on average, they needed 56. (III) Frankly, such optimism is one of the most charming flaws of the human mind. What the students failed to take into account was not their ability but the interruptions, illnesses and other setbacks that almost always occur.

    3The consequences are not limited to missed deadlines. When governments misjudge large projects, taxpayers pay the price. The Sydney Opera House, which was expected to be completed in 1963 at a cost of seven million Australian dollars, opened a decade late and cost over a hundred million. Had the planners examined how comparable buildings had progressed, they might have produced a more realistic estimate.

    4This is precisely the remedy that researchers recommend. Rather than asking how long this task should take, we ought to ask how long similar ones actually took. Known as taking the “outside view”, this approach replaces hopeful imagination with evidence. It will not eliminate delays altogether; nevertheless, it makes them far less likely to come as a shock.

    Yazma (MT1B)

    Yaklaşık 220 kelimelik tek bir akademik paragraf. WHO 3 (academic paragraph) ve WHO 4 (compare/contrast) kalıpları üzerinden.

    İskelet

    1. Topic sentence = konu + controlling idea. Sayı ver: two main ways / two major effects.
    2. Major 1 + 2–3 minor (açıklama, sebep, örnek, sonuç)
    3. Major 2 + 2–3 minor
    4. Concluding sentence: topic sentence'ı başka kelimelerle toparla, yeni fikir açma.

    Major'ları tanıtma (enumeration)

    The first difference is related to … / One advantage is that … / To begin with, …
    The second difference concerns … / Another reason is that … / Furthermore, …

    Minor'lara geçiş

    In other words, / That is, · This is because … · For example, / For instance, · As a result, / Consequently, · In addition, / Moreover,

    • Kelime sınırını aşmak ceza getirmez; altında kalmak Content'i düşürür.

    Örnek paragraf

    Soru: What are the differences between studying in one's hometown and in another city? Discuss two points of contrast. Altı çizili yerler iskelet, sarı yerler kalıp.

    Attending university in one's hometown differs from studying in another city in two main ways. The first difference is related to living costs. Students who stay in their hometown usually live with their families, so they do not have to pay rent or spend much on food. As a result, they can save their money for books or hobbies. In contrast, those who move to another city must cover accommodation, bills and daily meals, which often forces them to rely on scholarships or part-time jobs. The second difference concerns personal independence. Unlike students living at home, whose parents tend to handle most household tasks, students in a new city have to manage everything on their own. For example, they need to cook, do their laundry and plan a monthly budget. Although this can be stressful at first, it teaches them valuable life skills at an early age. Students who remain at home, on the other hand, may enjoy more comfort, but they have fewer opportunities to learn how to stand on their own feet. In conclusion, while studying in one's hometown is more economical and comfortable, moving to another city offers young people a greater chance to become independent adults.
